Dishes on the map

Three ways to float the illustrated dishes over Mexico's regions, using the six images made so far. Dishes sit near where they come from, which is often a city rather than a whole region. Hover a plate, or tap it on a phone.

One image per region, or several?One per region at country zoom. Several once you zoom into a region, placed at their cities.
Dishes from all over Mexico?Keep them off the map. They get an "Across Mexico" plate stack in the sea, and hovering one warms the whole country.
Which dishes show?The region's most popular one or two. Everything else stays in the list below the map.
A

Floating plates at the source

Every shown dish hovers over its home city on a thin stem, with a soft shadow on the land under it. Tacos al pastor over Mexico City, mole over Puebla, pozole over Guadalajara, mezcal over Oaxaca.

Works

  • Teaches geography directly: this dish comes from here.
  • Feels alive. The bob and shadow read as hovering, not pinned.

Breaks

  • Central Mexico is crowded: Mexico City and Puebla are 130 km apart, so their plates have to be pushed aside on stems.
  • At phone width the plates are about 34 px and start to overlap.
B

One signature plate per region

Each region shows the one dish it's most known for, sitting on its lettered area. A small count says there's more. Hovering fans out the rest. Regions without an image yet show an empty plate.

Works

  • Never crowded: at most five plates, one per area.
  • Matches the app's regions, so tapping a plate can open the region, just like tapping its name.

Breaks

  • Loses precision: mole and tacos both sit on "Central Mexico", not on Puebla and Mexico City.
  • Choosing one signature dish per region is an editorial call for every country.
C

Callout cards with leader lines

The images move off the land into the sea as small cards, each tied to its city by a line. Dishes from everywhere get a gold card, and hovering it outlines the whole country.

Works

  • No overlap problem: the sea has room, and every line still lands on the exact city.
  • Bigger images, closer to the tiles in the list.

Breaks

  • Covers the map's margins and reads like an annotated diagram, not a place.
  • Countries without much sea (landlocked, or tightly framed) have nowhere to put the cards.
Recommended

B at country zoom, A when you zoom in

Start with one plate per region, which stays calm even on a phone. Zooming into a region (the app's camera already does this) swaps to floating plates at their cities, where there's finally room for Mexico City and Puebla to sit apart.

Try both zoom levels, then hover the plates.
